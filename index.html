What I do

I build web interfaces and the AI workflows behind them. Today most of my work runs through AI agents — Webflow, WordPress and HubSpot client sites — under written rules, safety hooks and my own review.

My rule: deterministic code does the mechanics, models get language and judgement. On a live Webflow build that made a style lookup about 250× smaller than the MCP dump. When I changed heading levels across a live 280-page site, scripts compared every page before and after — 59 MB of HTML, more than a model can read at once — and showed me only the one difference worth checking.

Before that: two and a half years on a React/TypeScript SaaS product — its design system, complex inputs, and a Playwright test-data layer the frontend team builds on — and 19 Webflow sites built from Figma, from single pages to several hundred.

Selected work

cuewigs — I wrote the frontend. The GSAP implementation, the responsive system and the performance are mine; the design and the motion spec came from the designer. The layout scales as one continuous system: everything in rem, the root font-size interpolated against the viewport, so nothing jumps between breakpoints. That one was my call. Awwwards Developer Award and Site of the Day, and FWA Site of the Day, February 2024.

kasiawalter.it — this site. I designed it by directing AI tools through exploration, then built a real-time engine sharing a render loop with a React app: Three.js and TypeScript, custom geometry, additive sprite and line rendering, bloom post-processing. It holds on a mid-range phone — the GPU is tiered, effects are dropped by cost, scroll work is rAF-coalesced and layout is read once a frame. Profiling cut scroll jank from 6 frames over 24 ms to zero without removing an effect. Reduced motion switches the engine off and the page still reads.

Research: steering AI away from templated design — three successive agent skills, seven blind-ranked runs. Prohibitions in the prompt barely helped; an external randomised generator removed hard-coded layout values entirely (96 → 0).

Design-direction methodology — an agent skill of my own: research in four lenses, a semantic brief with an approval gate, reverse-reading as the selection step, and blind subagents generating competing directions. I wrote it because directing agents without a method produces confident, generic work.
